Word Graph — balls set inside the W

The W stays a solid letterform, so it survives shrinking. Three smaller balls sit inside its strokes as coloured joints, and the letter itself is what connects them — no extra edges needed. Fewer nodes (three, not five), and the alphabet still wins.

Why this fixes the previous version — measured, not assumed. At 16 px the strokes are 2.3 px wide and each ball 2.0 px across, so the letterform stays one unbroken mass. Sampling the rendered pixels at the ball centres gives:

All three hues survive — only the red loses saturation to antialiasing. I expected the ramp to be lost at this size and it isn't: three well-separated hues on a solid stroke still read as three colours, which is the opposite of the node-path W where the 2.5-unit edges rasterised to 0.6 px and the letter dissolved entirely. This variant therefore holds up at every size, header through favicon, which none of the earlier marks managed.
