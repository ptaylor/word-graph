Word Graph — a graph drawn inside the W

Same solid letterform as before, but now the three nodes are joined by a visible routed edge in a contrasting tone, so the shape reads as a small graph sitting in the letter rather than as three loose dots. Three ways to run that edge.

Why the graph has to follow the letter. I measured the white space inside the W before drawing anything, because an independent graph tucked into the counters would have been the more obvious reading:
Stroke widthLeft counter, widestMiddle counter, widest
9 units13.3 units12.3 units, at the baseline only
7 units15.3 units14.3 units, at the baseline only
5 units17.3 units16.3 units, at the baseline only
A node needs about 10 units of diameter to read at all. The counters are wedges that close to nothing at the peak, so even at a spindly 5-unit stroke two nodes side by side barely fit anywhere, and the middle wedge is only wide at the very bottom. There is simply no pocket in a W large enough to hold a graph of its own — which is why the letter's own strokes have to be the edges, traced in a second colour.

Weighting, measured. The routed edge is balance-critical — it is competing with the letter for the same pixels. Counting ink by colour:
VariantLetterRouteNodes
Inline47.6%41.0%11.4%
Route66.7%22.1%11.2%
Loop64.1%25.1%10.8%
The Inline variant traces the whole letter, so its route ends up nearly as heavy as the letter itself and the mark reads as a pale W with navy edging rather than a navy W with a line drawn inside it. Sampling confirms it: every stroke midpoint comes out at the route colour, including the outer strokes. The Route and Loop variants only trace the edges that actually exist, leaving the outer strokes solid navy.

And nothing is lost at small sizes. At 16 px the outer strokes still sample as pure navy (34, 53, 85) and the routed edge still samples as (161, 176, 196) — byte-identical to its value at 112 px. Unlike the node-path W, where the edges collapsed to 0.6 px and the letter dissolved, the letterform and the graph both survive all the way down to tab size.
