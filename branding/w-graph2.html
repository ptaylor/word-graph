Word Graph — thicker W, nodes offset in height

The recommended Route mark, rebuilt. The letterform goes from a 12-unit to a 15-unit stroke, and the red and green nodes come off the two bottom vertices so they sit at different heights. Because the nodes leave the vertices, the routed edge now runs right-elbow-apex-left: three segments, four bends — a route rather than a symmetric ∨∧.

Why the letter got wider, not just fatter. Stroke weight and the notch between the two top vertices pull against each other — the notch is just (top spacing − stroke width), and the top spacing is capped by the canvas. At 12 units in a 64-wide box the notch was 11 units; at 15 it would have collapsed to 8, and the outer vertices would have been left with under 1 unit of margin. So the box widened to 72 and the vertices spread with it, which buys a 10-unit notch and 3.5 units of padding at the heavier weight.



Verified at 16 px. Sampling the Fall build at tab size returns the routed edge at (161, 176, 196) and a plain stroke at (34, 53, 85) — both byte-identical to their values at 112 px — and the notch between the top vertices still samples pure white at (255, 255, 255). That last one is the important check: a heavier stroke is what can close a W up into a blob, and at 15 units it hasn't.

Node heights, measured from the render. Fall puts red at y=29.6 against green at y=44.0, a 14.4-unit drop; Rise mirrors it at 44.0 against 29.3; Shallow closes the gap to 32.5 against 39.3.
