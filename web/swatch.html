Colour & type for the graph

Node sizes are the real ones, measured from the app at zoom 1 — a “hotel” search at 3 changes (36 words) and 5 changes (566 words). Every word in a view is the same length, which is why the monospace option below makes every box exactly the same width.

1 · what is there now — rainbow, system-ui 600, dark navy label

Problems, measured: dark navy on these fills fails the 4.5:1 body-text contrast on four of seven colours — worst on #6d597a at 1.98:1 and #457b9d at 2.69:1 (even the root red is 2.97:1, under the 3:1 large-text floor). The white halo meant to rescue it is invisible on the pale fills (1.67:1 on #e9c46a). Orange → yellow collapses to a ΔE of 6.2 under deuteranopia, i.e. those two rings are the same colour to ~6% of men.

2 · the type — same sizes, three ways

Look at ring 3 at 36 words: . The mono face is the one that pulls its weight here: every word in a search is the same length, so all boxes become identical and the letters line up between rings — you can see which position changed, which is the whole point of a word ladder. It also has unambiguous l/I/1 and O/0.

3 · palette option A — fix in place

Same six hues, but the yellow (the one that vanishes against the orange) is replaced by a deeper amber, the teal is darkened, and each label picks white or navy by its own fill's luminance.

4 · palette option B — sequential, root deepest  ·  APPLIED

What is in the app now, with the root taking the same hue at its deepest stop rather than its own red. Distance is already encoded twice — by ring position and by node size — so colour does not have to carry it. Nothing to collide under any colour blindness: every adjacent pair holds a 9.5 dE separation under deuteranopia and protanopia alike, where the old orange-to-yellow step fell to 6.2. Worst label contrast across the ramp is 4.65:1, against 1.98:1 before.
